# Expert Methodology Knowledge Base

*How The Fantasy Footballers and Sal Vetri analyze — extracted from 28
transcripts (Aug 4-21, 2026) by subagent study. This file is about HOW they
think, not what they concluded (that's expert_takes.json). Append new
methods whenever a distillation pass reveals one; date additions.*

## Metrics they trust (and how they use them)

- **Target share as the master WR stat** — 25%+ good, 30%+ elite; cited before any yardage number, paired with red-zone target share for TD equity. [sal-vetri]
- **"Targets are forever" — target share is the stickiest year-over-year stat** — every 27%+ target-share player is priced top-12 except the ones they buy (Flowers, G. Wilson). [fantasy-footballers]
- **YPRR + first downs per route run as efficiency truth-serum** — "the two stickiest stats for a WR"; used to prove a down box-score year was environment, not decline. [both]
- **Target rate (targets/route) as a "first read" detector** — top-8 = "the play was designed for you." [sal-vetri]
- **Routes run per game as offense health** — a WR1 should run 30-32+; Waddle's 23.6 proved the offense was broken, not the player. Also catchable-target rate. [sal-vetri]
- **With/without + healthy-only splits as the core valuation tool** — JT 27 ppg with Daniel Jones vs 12.9 without; whole "wash" seasons excluded from evaluation. [sal-vetri]
- **Vacated-opportunity accounting** — track targets/carries leaving each team (GB 167 targets, JAX 72% of carries), then ask who is physically capable of absorbing them. [sal-vetri]
- **Vegas everywhere** — win totals as an RB screen (RBs rounds 1-3 on sub-7-win teams beat ADP only 30%); underdog-in-3+-of-first-4 as a slow-start screen; player yardage props as role proof. [both]
- **TD regression by LOCATION, not totals** — Etienne's 6 receiving TDs on 36 catches, McBride's 7 TDs from the 11-20; TDs stick when the role is renewed and volume rises. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Red-zone/10-zone conversion efficiency** — 15% TD rate inside the 10 is disqualifying; top-5 red-zone success = team trust. [both]
- **O-line-independent runner metrics** — YPC when not hit behind the line, yards after contact, broken tackles; yards BEFORE contact to blame scheme vs player. [sal-vetri]
- **Snap %, personnel groupings, route participation as gatekeepers** — in 2-WR sets or not; TE under ~70% snaps = "never fantasy relevant." [both]
- **Draft capital + contract money as revealed beliefs** — traded 1sts and guaranteed money outrank camp talk; vet-minimum signings are smoke. [both]
- **TE streaming thresholds (7-yr study)** — 17%+ target share (~20 ideal), 45+ yd/gm for top-10 (54+ top-6), 15%+ air-yard share. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Rookie-WR snap base rates by draft capital** — top-15 picks: 75% snaps wk 1-6; round 2: 48%/3.7 tgts; round 3: 30%/2.1 — day-2/3 rookie WRs are second-half waiver plays, not draft picks. [fantasy-footballers]

## Frameworks & heuristics

- **Busts are defined by price, not talent** — every fade carries "if he falls two rounds, take him." [both]
- **Opportunity-cost drop-off comparison for early QBs** — QB gap round 2 vs round 6 is 1-2 ppg; the RB cliff over the same span is huge. [sal-vetri]
- **Re-price rules when the market moves** — elite QBs fell to rounds 4-6 this year, so late-round-QB dogma is outdated; strategy rules are market-relative, never permanent. [fantasy-footballers]
- **RB dead zone as a moving market artifact** — the ADP pendulum swings toward last year's winning position; RB24 cost a 7th in 2024, a mid-5th now; the sin is opportunity cost. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Depth asymmetry** — RB injuries transfer value predictably (draft insurance RBs), WR injuries scatter it (solve WRs on waivers). "The team is built in August but finished in November." [fantasy-footballers]
- **"Take dumps now, not later"** — late fliers with no week-1 role get cut immediately (only 4 of ~60 rounds-11-15 picks mattered in month one last year); exception: insurance RBs. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Aging study + encore effect** — 10 oldest drafted players yearly: 30W-50L-20P; old WRs 21% hit rate; old QBs fine; a player who beat ADP *while old* is the one sanctioned old-guy buy. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Never dusty veterans late** — last-round picks must be young contingent upside; Sal's round map: R1-2 reliability, R3-5 WR feast/elite mobile QB, R6 BPA, R7-9 last solid RBs, R10-11 last quality QB, R13+ upside only. [both]
- **Two RBs early (this year)** — 2 in the first 3-4 picks, never 3 in 5 rounds. [both]
- **Draft-slot value = two-pick combos and turn optionality**, not the first pick alone. [sal-vetri]
- **Tier drafting with a dying-tier trigger; never chase a positional run** — "a QB run = WRs falling to you." [fantasy-footballers]
- **Injury actuarial rules** — ACL: 18-24 months to full burst (back-to-back ACLs near-disqualifying); hamstrings for RB/WR = high re-injury, worse with age; turf toe median 28 days; "seeking second opinions" is itself bad news. Pay attention to bad news, not good news. [both]
- **Coach/scheme priors move projections before stats do** — McDaniel motion, Reid RB1s ~1,400 yds, O'Connell QB resurrection, Nagy low-motion tax; scheme-fit splits (outside-zone YPC) justify buys. [both]
- **Preseason decoded by usage, not box scores** — who plays WITH starters and is pulled WITH starters vs who plays deep with backups; preseason only matters when it CONFIRMS an existing camp drumbeat (one-off heroes are Marquez Callaways). [both]
- **Camp-report filtering** — beat-writer USAGE notes (first-team reps, X designation, red-zone period) are signal; generic coach praise and hype aggregators are noise; big contested-catch WRs always look great unpadded (auto-discount). [sal-vetri]
- **Someone must eat on every bad offense** — 500+ attempt teams with empty WR rooms must produce a 100-target player going round 12+; free square, drop in two weeks if wrong. [both]
- **Good-offense/unclear-backfield late-round rule** — buy the offense before the player; underdog status hurts RBs but can HELP QBs (shootouts). [fantasy-footballers]

## Process & conviction habits

- **"My Guys" as rationed conviction** — 3 names per analyst per year, publicly claimed, locked as late as possible so August injuries don't strand them. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Consensus rankings, individually-owned deviations** — median consensus product + each host's outlier positions stated on record; disagreements become explicit falsifiable bets. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Quantified same-day risk updates** — news moves a rating ("a down arrow appears next to Tyson today"); suspensions handled by deleting 1-2 games and re-ranking. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Volume mock-drafting as market research** — 100+ drafts a summer; mock from YOUR slot on YOUR platform's ADP with if-then branch plans; deliberately explore builds you dislike. [both]
- **Public, reasoned take-changing** — "if you don't change your take when quality new information comes in, you're not doing this right." [sal-vetri]
- **ADP-efficiency rule for reaching** — the first ~5 rounds of ADP are historically accurate (reaches destroy value); round 5-6 is where it breaks open and gut picks are fine. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Sanctioned gut overrides — named out loud** — you may consciously break a rule, but must say which rule you're breaking. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Wipe the slate clean** — re-draft the player who burned you; last year's pain is not predictive. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Draft as late to kickoff as possible** — ideally after preseason week 3. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Curated sources, not a feed** — named beat writers, PFF snap charts, Reception Perception route charting, film confirmation. [both]

## Roster construction (added 2026-08-22)

- **Early-down heuristic for late RBs** — ~90% of RB opportunities (and ~80%
  of RB receiving points) come on early downs, so a strong early-down +
  goal-line role on a good offense outweighs a missing receiving profile
  when pricing end-of-draft RBs (their Jordan Mason case). [fantasy-footballers]
- **Target-share trajectory beats TD totals as the within-tier WR
  tiebreaker** — volume is sticky, TDs regress; a rising multi-year share
  (Flowers 24→25→28%) outranks a flat one. [fantasy-footballers]
- **Anchor-RB fragility principle** — once anchored on elite RBs, do NOT buy
  mid-round insurance RBs ("if Gibbs gets hurt I'm not winning anyway");
  spend those picks on a WR depth portfolio where one of three bench WRs
  hitting covers a fragile starter. [sal-vetri]
- **Sharp-room market read** — in expert leagues, onesie positions (QB/TE)
  fall well past home-league ADP while RB/WR get pushed up; plan to buy the
  falling QB/TE rather than reach at RB/WR turns. (Home leagues on
  Sleeper's list behave the opposite way — our platform-list rule stands.)
  [sal-vetri]
- **Pre-week-1 camp-insurance picks** — when drafting 3+ weeks out, spend
  final picks on direct handcuffs to injury-flagged starters purely to
  pre-empt waiver bidding, with a stated plan to drop them for K/DEF by
  week 1. Complements our week-1 cut discipline rule. [sal-vetri]

## Mechanism audit — verify the WHY, not just the WHO (added 2026-08-24)

A take bundles a **conclusion** ("Shaheed is underpriced") with a
**mechanism** ("because the gutted RB room forces Seattle to pass"). These
fail independently: a right conclusion can rest on a wrong mechanism. Check
them separately, and when a mechanism is testable against our data, test it
before repeating it — a mechanism restated as shorthand becomes an
established fact in the next document that cites it.

**Known-weak causal chains to challenge on sight:**

- **"Thin/injured backfield → team passes more → WR volume."** Mostly false
  for good teams. Pass rate is driven by *game script* (trailing teams
  throw), down/distance, and coaching philosophy — not RB quality. A team
  with a high Vegas win total leads more, and leading teams **run** more.
  Vacated carries get absorbed by whoever is next up, not converted to
  throws. Caught live 2026-08-24 on Sal's Shaheed take (SEA, 10.5-win
  total), and in our own writing on Reed (GB, 9.5) and Evans (SF, 9.5).
  The chain IS legitimate for genuinely bad teams — New Orleans at a 7.5-win
  total will trail and throw — so check the win total before accepting it.
- **The stronger argument is usually already in our data.** In all three
  cases above, `team_env.json` carried a far better reason than the
  narrative one: vacated TARGET share (GB 37.4%, SF 29.9%). Reach for the
  measured input over the story. If a take's stated mechanism is weak but
  a measured one supports the same conclusion, say so explicitly — swap the
  reasoning, keep the conclusion, and note the swap.
- **Cross-position claims need an explicit transmission channel.** Linking
  a RB's injury to a WR's value requires naming the channel (pass rate,
  target share, red-zone role) and checking it. "Same team" is not a
  mechanism.

## Presentation techniques

- **Every verdict is cost-anchored** — no take ends without the price and the reversal price. [sal-vetri]
- **Voice the naive league-mate, then rebut** — makes the mechanism memorable. [sal-vetri]
- **Conviction-graded vocabulary as bet sizing** — "bet my house" vs "take a gander, drop him in two weeks." [sal-vetri]
- **Named, reusable mnemonics** — dead zone, encore effect, facts-vs-feelings, stay water. Concepts get handles so they can be re-invoked in-season. [both]
- **Uncomfortable-math openers + counterpunch stats** — lead with a reframing base rate ("27% — the chance an RB plays a full season"). [fantasy-footballers]
- **Range-of-outcomes / no-downside framing for late picks** — reversible options: "if he stinks, drop him; if he hits, top-12; no downside in round 11." [both]

## Style difference in one line

Sal is a cost-arbitrage machine (one mispriced player at a time: splits +
vacated volume + role reports vs ADP); FF are base-rate actuaries
(decade-long studies applied as screens). They differ in delivery, not
doctrine — both converge on target share > box scores, vacated-opportunity
math, Vegas as ground truth, price-relative verdicts, and ruthless week-1
cut discipline.
