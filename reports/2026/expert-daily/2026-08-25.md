## Methodology corner

Nine new lessons appended to `expert_methods.md`. The three that change how we work:

**The three-outcome cap.** FF sort every pick into "guaranteed to produce on some level" versus wild cards whose range genuinely spans worthless / flex / league-winning — and then deliberately carry only **one or two wild cards per roster**. Our `league_winners` list flags contingent upside well but has no such cap; it will happily hand you five of them in the last four rounds. Their classification is also a hype filter: a player who *cannot* reach the league-winning end (Irving without the receiving role, Harvey behind two names) doesn't belong in the bucket at all.

**The stale-ADP trap.** An ADP formed during a player's bad-news window is obsolete the second the news flips, and every manager is staring at the same number hoping for the same steal. This is the ADP-side twin of our own rule 5, and it has a direct consequence for us: **a large positive Value gap on a recently-recovered player is partly an artifact, not an edge**. Nabers is the live case.

**Camp reception counts by starting quarterback.** A pecking-order metric that exists before any snap chart does, and one they trust over preseason completion percentage ("they should be failing in preseason"). It's the cleanest thing in today's haul that we could actually compute if the data were scrapeable.
